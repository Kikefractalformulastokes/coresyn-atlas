Status: ACTIVE COMMERCIAL DESIGN · not yet a paid validated product.
Domain Lead: Veronica Ramon — Sustainability, ESG & Climate Risk.
Source basis: UN Global Compact + BSR, Supply Chain Sustainability: A Practical Guide for Continuous Improvement (2010), treated as a historical/reference methodology, not as current law or certification.

## Why this line exists

The reference guide frames supply-chain sustainability as management of environmental, social and economic impacts plus good governance across the lifecycle of goods and services. It recommends a practical system built around business case, vision, supplier expectations, scope, risk mapping, supplier engagement, internal governance, collaboration, targets, measurement and reporting.

CoreSyn converts that management logic into an evidence-assurance product for 2026.

## Commercial product

### CORESYN SUPPLY CHAIN EVIDENCE GATE

Buyer questions:
- Which suppliers and sub-tiers carry the material ESG / climate / operational risk?
- Which claims about supplier sustainability are actually evidenced?
- Which supplier KPIs are self-reported, independently verified, stale, inconsistent or missing?
- Where should procurement focus limited assurance resources?
- Can a supplier improvement or decarbonisation programme be defended to investors, customers, auditors or funders?

CoreSyn workflow:
SUPPLY CHAIN MAP → CATEGORY / SPEND / CRITICALITY → COUNTRY / PROCESS / SUB-TIER → ESG RISK EVENTS → PROBABILITY × SEVERITY → EVIDENCE QUALITY → SUPPLIER SEGMENTATION → EXPECTATIONS / CODE → SELF-ASSESSMENT → DOCUMENT EVIDENCE → AUDIT / THIRD-PARTY EVIDENCE WHERE AVAILABLE → CORRECTIVE ACTION → KPI / TARGET → TREND → GATE → PROCUREMENT / HUMAN DECISION.

Gate states:
PASS / CONDITIONAL / HOLD / FAIL / NOT TESTED.

## Risk segmentation

Use risk as a prioritisation mechanism, not as an unsupported universal score.
Relevant dimensions from the source methodology include:
- risk to society
- risk to the company
- supplier spend
- geography / country context
- category criticality
- tier / sub-tier
- nature of operation / opacity
- probability of event
- severity of consequence

Any numerical CoreSyn score must be separately validated before being sold as predictive or calibrated risk.

## Supplier engagement ladder

1. Set expectations.
2. Request structured self-assessment / evidence.
3. Review evidence and contradictions.
4. Prioritise suppliers requiring deeper review.
5. Define corrective actions and deadlines.
6. Support capability-building where useful.
7. Reassess and measure progress.
8. Escalate or recommend human procurement action when zero-tolerance or repeated unresolved gaps exist.

CoreSyn does not replace qualified social auditors, legal counsel, certification bodies, occupational-safety experts or procurement authority.

## Source-use guardrail

The 2010 UN Global Compact / BSR guide is useful as a historical architecture and examples source. It must not be represented as current 2026 regulation, a certification standard, or proof that an approach is legally sufficient today. Current legal / standards claims must be verified against current primary sources before client delivery.

## Canonical positioning

CoreSyn Supply Chain Evidence Gate does not ask only: “Does the supplier have a sustainability policy?”
It asks: “What evidence supports the decision to trust this supplier, this KPI, this corrective action or this claim?”

Proof before trust.
